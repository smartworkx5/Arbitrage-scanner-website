Funding-rate cash-and-carry

A delta-neutral approach that aims to collect perpetual futures funding payments.

How it works

Perpetual futures have no expiry date. To keep their price close to spot, exchanges use a funding rate: periodically, one side of the market pays the other. When the rate is positive, traders holding long positions pay traders holding short positions.

In a cash-and-carry setup you buy the asset on the spot market and open an equal-size short in the perpetual. Price moves on the two sides largely offset, so the position is delta-neutral, and the aim is to collect the funding payments.

What the scanner shows

The funding-rate scanner lists rates across markets so you can compare the potential carry on different assets side by side.

What can go wrong

  • Funding can change. A positive rate today may fall or turn negative, which reverses the direction of payments.
  • Fees. Opening and closing both legs costs money, so the carry has to outlast those costs.
  • Margin and liquidation. The short side needs collateral. A sharp price rise can put the short under pressure even though the overall position is hedged.
  • Basis moves. Spot and perpetual prices can drift apart temporarily.

"Delta-neutral" does not mean "risk-free". Study the mechanics on the paper trading bot before committing real capital.
