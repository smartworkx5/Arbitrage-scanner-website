Fees, slippage and transfer time: what turns a spread into a loss

The three costs every arbitrage trade has to beat, with a checklist you can apply to any opportunity.

Updated 30 September 2026

A price gap is only the starting point. Three costs decide whether it becomes profit.

1. Fees

Most exchanges charge a fee on every trade. A buy and a sell means two fees, and a triangular loop means three. On a small spread, that is often the whole margin.

2. Slippage

The price you see is for a small quantity. Larger orders consume several levels of the order book, or move a pool price, so the average fill is worse than the quote.

3. Transfer time and cost

Moving coins between exchanges costs a network fee and takes time. Prices can move while you wait. This is why zero-transfer triangular loops are interesting: they avoid this cost entirely, though they have others.

A quick checklist

  • Write down the buy price and the sell price.
  • Subtract fees on every leg.
  • Estimate slippage for your real trade size.
  • Subtract transfer or gas costs.
  • Ask what happens if the price moves against you before the last leg.
